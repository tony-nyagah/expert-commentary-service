# Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDT) EQA Program Guidelines

## Clinical Relevance
Malaria remains one of the most fatal parasitic infections globally, primarily caused by *Plasmodium falciparum*, *Plasmodium vivax*, *Plasmodium malariae*, and *Plasmodium ovale*. Rapid, accurate diagnostic results are vital for patient outcomes. The Malaria EQA program evaluates a laboratory's ability to detect Malaria antigens (such as HRP2 and pLDH) in blood specimens using immunochromatography (rapid test cassettes).

## Testing Principles & Methodology
- **Target Antigens**: 
  - **HRP2 (Histidine-Rich Protein II)**: Specific to *Plasmodium falciparum*.
  - **pLDH (Plasmodium Lactate Dehydrogenase)**: Common to all *Plasmodium* species (pan-antigen).
- **Mechanism**: Lateral flow immunochromatography using capillary or venous whole blood specimens.

## Standard EQA Sample Handling & Protocol
1. **Specimen Reception**: Inspect sample vials for leakage or discoloration.
2. **Reconstitution (if lyophilized)**: Follow reconstitution instructions exactly. Pipetting inaccuracy directly affects antigen concentration.
3. **Buffer Volume**: Dispense the exact number of buffer drops recommended by the manufacturer. Insufficient buffer volume results in incomplete sample clearance and background staining, making lines faint or illegible.
4. **Incubation Time**: Read the test strip exactly at the specified time (usually 15–20 minutes). Reading too early causes false negatives due to incomplete migration; reading too late causes false positives due to nonspecific binding.
5. **Quality Control**: Ensure the Control Line (C) is clearly visible before interpreting patient lines (T1, T2).

## Typical Failure Modes & Corrective Actions
- **Faint Test Lines**: Often caused by reading under poor lighting, reading too early, or using degraded/expired cassettes. Corrective Action: Maintain designated timers and ensure test area has adequate lux lighting.
- **Nonspecific Background Stain**: Caused by reading after the incubation window or using an incorrect buffer. Corrective Action: Set a physical timer and use ONLY the manufacturer-supplied buffer.
- **Inconsistent Reagent Quality**: Unverified kits can yield high false negative/positive rates. Corrective Action: Pre-verify every batch against internal control panels before clinic deployment.
